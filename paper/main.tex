\title{Does Symbolic Grounding Inoculate LLM Agents Against Conformity?}
\ifanon

  \author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Bidit Das}}
\fi
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent debate can cause \emph{harmful conformity}. An agent gives a correct
answer, then drops it under peer pressure. Almost all evidence for this comes from
debate in natural language, where a peer's claim cannot be checked mechanically. We
ask a diagnostic question. Is conformity a property of the debated \emph{medium} or
of the \emph{model}? We hold the task, the agents, and the fabricated peer pressure
fixed. We vary one factor: whether the debated object is prose or an executable
program the agent can run. The sample is \NItems{} GSM8K items and \NAgents{} agents,
giving \NPairsAgentItem{} item-agent pairs observed in both media. Excess harmful
conformity was \ExcessNLPct\% in prose and \ExcessCodePct\% in code. The
pre-registered contrast was exactly zero: $\Delta=\DeltaPct$\%, with a 95\%
cluster-bootstrap CI of \DeltaCIPct{} points. We therefore find no evidence that
symbolic grounding protects against conformity. Why the test is weak matters more
than the null. The retained agents answered \PoolGSMNLPct\% of the primary pool
correctly before any pressure was applied. The beneficial-revision arm is then empty
by construction. Harmful flips under pressure numbered \HCNLShamK{} in prose and
\HCCodeShamK{} in code, over \NPairsAgentItem{} pairs per medium. Of those,
\McNNLB{} per medium were sham-only. The detectability floor for this design is
\MinFlips{} sham-only flips. We screened two harder pools. GSM-Hard is also at ceiling. Only a
\PoolMMLUNLItems{}-item quantitative MMLU-Pro probe fell below it, on too few items
to establish headroom. We report the null, the saturation that bounds it, and the
pool screening. Saturation is a validity threat for conformity studies in general. A
benchmark on which agents are almost never wrong cannot show what peer pressure does
to agents who are.
\end{abstract}

multi-agent debate, conformity, neurosymbolic verification, LLM agents

\section{Introduction}

Multi-agent debate (MAD) is widely proposed as a way to improve the reasoning of
large language models. Agents answer independently, read each other's solutions, and
revise. The assumed mechanism is error correction. A growing body of evidence
complicates that assumption. Debate also induces \emph{harmful conformity}, in which
an agent that answered correctly abandons its answer once peers disagree. Debate also
fails to reliably beat plain majority voting~\cite{choi2025debatevote}.

Two observations motivate this paper.

First, measuring peer pressure is harder than it looks. Hu et al.~\cite{speaker2026}
show that much of the conformity reported by peer-pressure benchmarks survives
\emph{removing the speaker}. Simply being asked to reconsider moves models, whatever
the peer said. A raw revision rate therefore confounds the peer's influence with the
invitation to revise. Any causal claim about peer pressure must be an \emph{excess}
over a matched no-pressure control. We adopt that as our outcome.

Second, there is a gap in what has been manipulated. The evidence above is drawn
almost entirely from debate conducted in \emph{natural language}. There a peer's
assertion cannot be checked mechanically. An agent facing a confident prose argument
can only defer or stay stubborn. But agents increasingly debate objects that are
\emph{executable}, such as programs, queries, and proofs. An agent can verify those
symbolically rather than socially. This raises a diagnostic question the existing
literature does not answer:

\begin{quote}
Is harmful conformity a property of the \emph{medium} or of the \emph{model}? If the
object under debate can be checked by running it, does conformity decrease?
\end{quote}

We test this by holding the task, the agents, and the peer pressure fixed, and by
varying only the medium. Pressure is delivered by \emph{fabricated} peers rather than
live models. Each item carries two peers that confidently assert the same wrong
answer. In the executable condition the peers also supply programs that run and print
exactly that wrong answer. Making pressure a fixed experimental material removes
peer-behaviour variance from the contrast. That is what makes the two media
comparable at all.

Our contribution is diagnostic, not architectural. We do not propose a debate
framework. We claim novelty for none of the following: deciding when to debate is
established~\cite{minority2025,imad2025}, recovering correct minorities is
established~\cite{sentinel2026}, and measuring conformity is
established~\cite{speaker2026}. What we add is an experiment in which the debated
medium is the manipulated variable, with the pre-registered contrast $\Delta$ defined
in Section~\ref{sec:outcome}.

The paper in brief:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Problem.} Debate can talk a correct agent out of a correct answer. It
is not known whether this depends on the medium in which agents debate.
\item \textbf{Method.} A pre-registered within-item, within-agent factorial on
GSM8K. Medium (prose or executable code) is crossed with pressure (no peer, or two
fabricated peers asserting the same wrong answer). The outcome is harmful conformity
in excess of the matched no-peer arm.
\item \textbf{Result.} The pre-registered contrast is exactly zero, with an interval
too wide to exclude a moderate effect. The binding cause is benchmark saturation:
initial accuracy of \PoolGSMNLPct\% empties the beneficial arm by construction and
starves the harmful one.
\item \textbf{Next.} Rerun the released protocol on a pool with measured headroom,
and on agents from more than one model family.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Does debate help, and when?} Choi et al.~\cite{choi2025debatevote} compare
debate against voting directly. They find debate's advantage is far from universal.
This has motivated \emph{selective} debate. Eo et al.~\cite{minority2025} debate only
when necessary, and iMAD~\cite{imad2025} learns when debate is worth its inference
cost. These methods decide \emph{whether} to invoke debate. We take the medium of
debate as given and ask what the medium itself does to the agents. Our question is
therefore orthogonal to theirs rather than competing with it.

\textbf{Conformity and its measurement.} The finding that closes off a naive version
of our own experiment is Hu et al.~\cite{speaker2026}. A large share of apparent
conformity in peer-pressure benchmarks persists when the peer is removed. That makes
unadjusted revision rates uninterpretable as peer influence. We therefore never
report a bare flip rate as evidence of pressure. Every pressure claim in this paper
is a difference against a no-pressure arm that is byte-identical except for the peer
block.

\textbf{Minority influence.} Minority Sentinel~\cite{sentinel2026} addresses when a
dissenting minority should overturn a majority. That work asks how to
\emph{aggregate} disagreement well. We ask whether an individual agent's
susceptibility to a majority depends on whether the majority's claim is checkable.

\textbf{The gap.} In all of the above, the debated object is natural language. To our
knowledge the medium has not been manipulated experimentally while task, agents, and
pressure are held fixed. That is the manipulation we report. We state this as a gap
in the literature we surveyed, not as a claim of priority.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Design}
The design is a within-item, within-agent factorial on GSM8K. Medium $\in$ \{natural
language, executable code\} is crossed with pressure $\in$ \{no-pressure, sham
peer\}. Each agent first answers independently in a given medium. It is then shown a
revision prompt that is byte-identical across pressure conditions except for the peer
block.

\subsection{Sham peer stimuli}
Peer pressure is a \emph{fixed experimental material}, not a live model. Each item
carries two fabricated peers that confidently assert the same wrong answer $W \neq
\text{gold}$. In the executable medium the peers also supply programs that
\emph{run} and print exactly $W$. Fixing pressure as a material removes
peer-behaviour variance from the contrast and makes the two media comparable.

Every stimulus passed automated validation before use. The checks were: $W \neq$
gold, both prose peers assert $W$, and both peer programs execute in a sandbox and
print exactly $W$. \NStimuli{} items passed. Failures were discarded, never repaired
by hand.

\subsection{Agents}
We use \NAgents{} models at temperature $0$ with an identical system prompt
(\texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, \texttt{claude-opus-4-5}). Three agents were
pre-registered. The mid-tier model was removed before any outcome quantity was
computed. In the primary run log, \RefusalAgentK{} of its \RefusalAgentCalls{} calls
returned a refusal stop reason, with output truncated mid-sentence on benign
arithmetic. The two retained models did so in \RetainedRefusals{} of
\RetainedCalls{} calls. The rate was also strongly differential across media:
\RefusalAgentCodePct\% of \RefusalAgentCodeN{} calls in the code medium against
\RefusalAgentNLPct\% of \RefusalAgentNLN{} calls in prose. That imbalance would bias
the primary estimand through differential missingness. The decision was taken during
the first execution pass and used stop-reason patterns only. It is recorded with its
first-pass counts in \texttt{DEVIATIONS.md} (D3); the figures quoted here are final
log totals, which include the pipeline's automatic retries. Single-family agents are
adequate for a within-agent contrast because the peer is fabricated. This limits
external validity, and we state it as a limitation rather than a strength.

\subsection{Item pools and difficulty screening}\label{sec:pools}
The primary pool is the pre-registered GSM8K sample (seed 20260914, frozen before any
model call). The pressure effect is defined on initially-correct pairs. A pool's
\emph{initial} accuracy therefore bounds what the design can observe. At ceiling
there are no initially-wrong pairs, so the beneficial direction is unmeasurable. We
report initial accuracy for every pool we examined (Table~\ref{tab:pools}), including
one we abandoned. Pools were screened on measured initial accuracy. That quantity
exists before any pressure condition is run, and therefore before any conformity
outcome exists. GSM-Hard was additionally filtered on ground-truth sanity
(positive-integer targets), which is a property of the item label rather than of
model behaviour.

\subsection{Outcome}\label{sec:outcome}
Harmful conformity (HC) is defined only on pairs \emph{initially correct} in that
medium: $\mathrm{HC}=1$ if the revised answer is incorrect. Excess harmful conformity
isolates the causal effect of the peer block,
\[ \mathrm{excess\_HC}(m) = \mathrm{HC}(\text{sham},m) - \mathrm{HC}(\text{none},m), \]
and the single pre-specified primary estimand is
\[ \Delta = \mathrm{excess\_HC}(\text{nl}) - \mathrm{excess\_HC}(\text{code}). \]
H1 predicts $\Delta > 0$. Inference is by a cluster bootstrap over items (10{,}000
resamples, seed 20260914) that resamples items and carries all agents within an item.
An exact McNemar test paired on (item, agent) is reported in support. The design,
outcome and analysis were frozen in \texttt{PREREGISTRATION.md} before any outcome
data existed.

The realised dataset is \NItems{} items $\times$ \NAgents{} agents
$=\NPairsAgentItem{}$ item-agent pairs. Each pair is fully observed in all six phases
(\NPairs{} item-agent-medium observations). \NExcl{} pair-arm cells were excluded by
the pre-registered rules and are listed with reasons in \texttt{exclusions.csv}. Of
these, \ExclRemovedAgent{} are the removed agent (D3). The remaining
\ExclRevisionMissing{} are revision cells never run, because execution stopped at a
hard token stop in frozen-sample order, below the session inference ceiling (D4).

Figure~\ref{fig:main} summarises both findings.

\textbf{The primary contrast is exactly zero.} Excess harmful conformity was
\ExcessNLPct\% in prose (\HCNLShamK{}/\HCNLShamN{} pairs harmed under sham pressure
against \HCNLNoneK{}/\HCNLNoneN{} with no peer). It was \ExcessCodePct\% in the
executable medium (\HCCodeShamK{}/\HCCodeShamN{} against
\HCCodeNoneK{}/\HCCodeNoneN{}). The pre-registered estimand is therefore $\Delta =
\DeltaPct$\%, with a 95\% cluster-bootstrap CI of \DeltaCIPct{} percentage points.
The supporting exact McNemar tests are $p = \McNNLP{}$ in prose ($b=\McNNLB{}$,
$c=\McNNLC{}$) and $p = \McNCodeP{}$ in code ($b=\McNCodeB{}$, $c=\McNCodeC{}$). H1
predicted $\Delta > 0$, and it is not supported. The interval is symmetric about zero
with a half-width of \DeltaCIHalfPct{} percentage points. This experiment therefore
does not distinguish ``grounding does not help'' from ``grounding helps by an amount
smaller than \DeltaCIHalfPct{} points''. We do not claim equivalence between the media.

\textbf{Pressure did move agents, in both media, and rarely.} Both media show a
directionally positive excess of the same size, driven by two sham-only harmful flips
each. In the executable medium one further pair flipped in the no-pressure arm
(\HCCodeNonePct\% baseline). Re-asking alone therefore destabilises a correct
program-derived answer occasionally. That is the speaker-free effect which motivates
the control arms~\cite{speaker2026}. One detail separates conformity from mere
destabilisation. Under code pressure \HCCodeShamPct\% of pairs were harmed, but only
\ToWCodeShamPct\% adopted the peers' answer $W$. One agent thus abandoned a correct
answer without accepting the peers' alternative. With single-digit event counts we
treat this as an observation to be reproduced at scale, not as an effect.

\textbf{The design's own sensitivity bounds the null.} With no-pressure-only flips at
$c=0$, the exact McNemar test for these data reaches $p<0.05$ only at
$b=\MinFlips{}$ sham-only flips. We observed $b=\McNNLB{}$ in each medium. The null
is thus a statement about an experiment that could not have detected an effect of the
magnitude plausibly present. We report it as inconclusive, not as evidence of
absence.

\textbf{The binding cause is benchmark saturation.} Table~\ref{tab:pools} gives the
reason the event counts are so small. The retained roster answered \PoolGSMNLPct\% of
the primary pool correctly in prose and \PoolGSMCodePct\% in code \emph{before any
pressure}, over all \PoolGSMNLN{} graded initial responses (\NStimuli{} validated
items $\times$ \NAgents{} agents, a superset of the pairs that completed every
phase). Two consequences follow. The first is mechanical. The beneficial-revision arm
is empty: there were no initially-wrong pairs, so the wrong$\to$right direction has
$n=0$ and cannot be estimated at all. That is why this paper can speak only to the
harmful direction. The second is methodological. A saturated pool also compresses the
harmful direction. Every pair is at risk, but a capable model that solves an item
outright is also the model least likely to be talked out of it. Screening confirmed
that this is not specific to GSM8K. GSM-Hard, whose items are GSM8K prose with
large-valued arithmetic, was also at or near ceiling (\PoolHardNLPct\% prose,
\PoolHardCodePct\% code). That is itself a small negative result about GSM-Hard as a
difficulty intervention for current models. A quantitative MMLU-Pro probe was the
only screen below ceiling, at \PoolMMLUNLK{} of \PoolMMLUNLN{} graded responses over
\PoolMMLUNLItems{} items. That indicates where headroom might be sought, and is far
too small to estimate it. Running that probe under pressure required multiple-choice
prompting the validated pipeline did not implement, and the remaining inference budget
did not permit a six-phase run within the submission window (D5). The pre-registered
cross-domain hypothesis H3 is therefore \emph{untested}, not null.

\textbf{Manipulation check.} In the executable medium the programs agents wrote agreed
with their stated answers in \ExecConsistPct\% of initial responses. The grounding
manipulation was therefore live rather than nominal: agents in the code condition
really were reasoning through artefacts whose output they could read. Sham peer
programs likewise executed and printed exactly $W$ by construction. Deference in that
arm cannot be explained by peers whose code visibly failed. The full experiment
consumed \TotalTokens{} tokens.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Single model family.} Both retained agents come from one provider's family.
What we observe may be a property of that family's post-training rather than of the
media. This is the most serious threat to external validity in the study. It is also
the most direct extension, because the design transfers unchanged to a cross-family
roster.

\textbf{Fabricated peers are not live agents.} Our peers are fixed materials. They
are always confident, always unanimous, and always wrong. This buys the clean causal
contrast that motivates the design, at the cost of realism. Real debate partners are
sometimes right, sometimes hedged, and sometimes internally inconsistent. Our
estimate is therefore the effect of \emph{unanimous confident wrong} peer pressure.
That is plausibly an upper bound on the harmful conformity a real debate would
induce.

\textbf{We measure only the harmful direction, and partly not by choice.} Every sham
peer is wrong, so harmful conformity is defined on initially-correct pairs.
\emph{Beneficial} conformity, in which an initially-wrong agent is moved right by a
peer, is outside the design. Saturation then removed even the weaker within-design
comparison. With initial accuracy at \PoolGSMNLPct\%, the wrong$\to$right arm has
$n=0$ and no beneficial rate can be computed. A full accounting of whether debate
pays requires both directions. Nothing here licenses the conclusion that debate is
net harmful in either medium.

\textbf{H3 was not tested.} The pre-registered cross-domain hypothesis required
running the pressure conditions on non-executable pools (ARC-Challenge, MMLU-Pro). No
pressure condition was run on any pool other than GSM8K. What we report instead is a
difficulty screening of candidate pools (Section~\ref{sec:pools}). H3 should be read
as untested. Treating it as a null result would misrepresent the evidence.

\textbf{One task, one round.} The primary contrast uses GSM8K arithmetic and a single
revision round. Arithmetic is where the executable medium is most naturally
checkable, so it is a favourable setting for the grounding hypothesis rather than a
neutral one. Tasks whose code is harder to verify may behave differently. Multi-round
dynamics, where conformity could compound, are not measured.

\textbf{Medium is not a single manipulation.} The executable condition differs from
the prose condition in more than checkability. The answer is produced by a different
process, the response format differs, and initial accuracy may differ between media.
We condition on initial correctness within medium so that the excess is computed on
comparable pairs. Even so, we cannot rule out that some of $\Delta$ reflects these
accompanying differences rather than checkability as such.

\textbf{Exclusions and infrastructure artefacts.} \NExcl{} item-agent pair-arm cells
were excluded by pre-registered rules (unparseable final answers, execution failures,
API errors). They are reported with reasons in \texttt{exclusions.csv} rather than
silently dropped, and we report exclusion counts per arm so that differential
attrition is visible. Some responses were also shortened or refused by platform-side
response handling during piloting. We logged that as a threat to validity rather than
treating the resulting records as clean.

\textbf{Sample size and power.} \NStimuli{} items passed stimulus validation and
\NItems{} completed all six phases, against a pre-registered target of about 100.
Both shortfalls were caused by a session inference ceiling: the first during stimulus
construction, the second by a hard token stop during execution. Neither involved
selection on outcomes. Items were frozen before any outcome data existed, execution
proceeded in frozen-sample order, and no item was dropped after its responses were
inspected (D1, D4). The consequence is severe. As Section~\ref{sec:outcome}
quantifies, the realised design needs \MinFlips{} sham-only flips for conventional
significance and observed \McNNLB{}. This study should be read as a working,
pre-registered instrument and a bound on effect size. It is not a decisive test of
the grounding hypothesis.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
We asked whether harmful conformity in multi-agent debate is a property of the medium
or of the model. We tested it by holding task, agents, and fabricated peer pressure
fixed while varying only whether the debated object was prose or an executable
program. On the realised sample the answer is that we cannot tell. Excess harmful
conformity was \ExcessNLPct\% in prose and \ExcessCodePct\% in code, and the
pre-registered contrast was exactly zero with an interval spanning \DeltaCIPct{}
points. Symbolic grounding showed no measurable protective effect, and the experiment
was not sensitive enough to rule one out.

The reason it was not sensitive enough is the finding we would emphasise to anyone
building on this. Conformity is defined on cases where an agent is right and might be
talked out of it, or wrong and might be talked into being right. Both require a
benchmark where models are sometimes wrong. On GSM8K the retained models were right
\PoolGSMNLPct\% of the time before any pressure. GSM-Hard, the standard
harder-arithmetic variant, was no better in this respect. Saturation is therefore not
a nuisance to note in passing. It is a first-order design constraint for conformity
and debate studies on current models: pool difficulty has to be screened, and
reported, before pressure is applied.

\textbf{What we would run next.} The instrument is the transferable part of this
work. The protocol, stimuli and analysis run unchanged on a different pool and a
different roster, so three steps follow directly. First, rerun the contrast on a pool
with measured headroom, selected by the same pre-pressure screen used here, which
points to quantitative multiple-choice items rather than harder arithmetic. Second,
replace the single-family roster with agents from several model families, which tests
whether conformity tracks post-training rather than medium. Third, add the beneficial
direction by including sham peers that are correct, which the present design cannot
observe at all. We release the pre-registration, the deviation log, the validated
sham stimuli, and the analysis code so that these runs can reuse the instrument
rather than rebuild it.
